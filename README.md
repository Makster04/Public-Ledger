# Public Ledger

A local, automatically refreshed scorecard of 2026 Senate and House candidates, with a Cloudflare Worker and D1 configuration for later hosting. The primary grading interface now shows Your Match, Integrity, and People First. Integrity combines reviewed promises as Honesty with Personal Finances, Fundraising, and a full Legal Trouble deduction. Its interface migration is complete, but its provisional methodology remains uncalibrated and the monthly comparison log continues separately. Historical Ethics and Promises Delivered grades remain preserved and unchanged. See the [complete score and index methodology](Scores/00_POLITICAL_SCORECARD_METHODOLOGY.md) and [Integrity specification](Scores/03_INTEGRITY_SCORE.md).

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 22.13 or later. On a Mac (or Linux), open Terminal in this folder, run `npm ci` the first time, then `npm start`, and visit **http://127.0.0.1:8787**. On Windows, open **Start Public Ledger.cmd** instead; it installs dependencies if needed. Python 3 is needed only for the by-hand import scripts listed under "Not refreshed automatically".

The local server binds only to your computer. It uses `.local/records.sqlite` and checks the refresh queue once a minute while running, fetching up to 40 source pages each time (`LOCAL_STEPS` in `.dev.vars`). Each source gets only its own share of those, set under its published or reported rate limit (`perRun` in `src/sources.mjs`), and a job that has started fetching pages finishes before new ones start. The hosted worker fetches 8 pages per run. Closing it stops the checks; saved data survives restarts. Nothing has been deployed or subscribed to.

## Enable automatic updates

1. Get the free or optional keys below for the sources and offline tools you want. The app runs without them.
2. Copy `.dev.vars.example` to `.dev.vars` in this directory. Every key in the example is blank.
3. Enter each key after its name. Keep this file private; Git ignores it.
4. Restart the local server. Open **API updates** to see which keys are set and each API's progress, and **Website updates** for the official websites read directly.

Set source keys are used by their respective refreshes; the OpenAI key is used only by the offline statement–action classifier command. Sources whose key is missing wait on the Updates screen as "Setup needed", and the app shows the reviewed research. Frontend code never receives the OpenAI key, and saved request links never include any key.

## What refreshes

**Money**

- **Campaign totals:** FEC principal committee summaries, on their own reporting dates.
- **Donors by employer:** FEC itemized individual contributions (over $200) totaled by the donor's employer, in each election cycle back to 2016, then month by month for the top 20 employers (this cycle's) back to 2016, for the top-20 donor tracking. Each earlier cycle's top 10 are kept too. Retired, self-employed and similar entries are left out. Donors' names are never kept or shown, as FEC rules on reusing contributor data require.
- **Donors by gift size:** the FEC's five size groups for the campaign committee's individual contributions in the current cycle ($200 and under, $200.01–$499.99, $500–$999.99, $1,000–$1,999.99, $2,000 and over; each gift counts by its own amount, not the donor's total), checked against the committee's itemized and unitemized totals (`public/donor-sizes.js`). Gifts reported only as a total are in the first group, counted once; itemized gifts the FEC hasn't sorted by size yet, from a newly filed report, are shown on their own. Drawn as a second bar on the Overview's money card and as a table under Money & Influence. Each earlier cycle from 2016 on is kept too (`cycles`, read once a cycle has ended), for the Big Money Independence tab's periods; the Big Money Independence grade's Donor part uses the sizes.
- **More campaign money from the FEC** (`public/fec-money.js`, same key), for the Big Money Independence tab:
  - **PAC contributions by cycle** (`pac-history`, weekly): every PAC contribution the FEC has processed (Form 3, line 11C), each cycle from 2016 on, with each PAC's type from the FEC's record of it. The current cycle's reviewed PAC table stays in `public/research.json`; a profile without one has its PAC money sorted by type from these lines.
  - **PAC money not yet processed** (`pac-efile`, daily): PAC contributions in the raw e-filings dated after the FEC's processed totals end, each transaction once (an amended report replaces the original). Shown with a * and not counted until the FEC processes it.
  - **Donors by state** (`donor-states`, weekly): itemized gifts from individuals by the donor's state, each cycle; in-state, out-of-state and the top states. The FEC has no address for smaller gifts. Shown only.
  - **Joint fundraising, credited to donors** (`joint-fundraising`, weekly): line 12 transfers from joint fundraising committees, with the memo entries that list each contributor's share, added up by the size of each share (donors' names are never kept); the part no entry lists is shares of $200 or less, counted as small gifts; committees' shares stay committee money. Big Money Independence's Donor part credits this money to the people who gave it. A cycle with more than 6,000 records is marked partial and not credited back.
  - **Gifts from the crypto and AI industry's people** (`industry-gifts`, weekly, `public/industries.js`): for each cycle, the FEC's itemized gifts to the campaign from people who give a listed company as their employer (searched by employer, ten names a request), and from the listed executives (searched by name, then matched by last name, first name and an employer on their list). Only matching gifts are kept, as company, executive, amount and date; no other donor's name is saved. The lists were approved on 2026-11-08: crypto super PACs Fairshake, Defend American Jobs, Protect Progress and Fellowship PAC; AI super PACs Leading the Future, Think Big, American Mission and Public First's Jobs and Democracy PAC and Defending Our Values PAC; crypto companies Coinbase, Ripple, Kraken, Gemini, Circle, Galaxy Digital, Consensys, Uniswap Labs, Paradigm, Jump Trading and the Blockchain Association; AI companies OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Scale AI, Perplexity, Databricks, CoreWeave, Nvidia and Palantir; Andreessen Horowitz under both; and 30 executives shown by name. Big Tech isn't counted as AI. The super PACs' spending comes from the outside-spending records on file (`out.cms`), matched by FEC committee ID or exact name. Shown in the Crypto and Big Tech + AI trackers on the Big Money Independence tab (see Trackers below); no score uses it.
  - **Super PACs** (`super-pacs`, daily, one site-wide check, `public/super-pacs.js`): the FEC's totals of independent expenditures by committee and candidate for the cycle (`schedules/schedule_e/by_candidate`), page by page; then each spender's committee type (`committees`, 100 a request), so super PACs (committee types O, V and W) are told apart from parties, ordinary PACs and groups filing on their own; then the largest gifts (one page of receipts, largest first) to the 50 biggest super PACs: companies, unions and other groups by name, people added up without names. Served at `/api/superpacs` and shown on the Super PACs page: each super PAC with its total, what it spent for and against each candidate (linked to their profile when there is one), and its largest gifts.
  - **Periods:** the Big Money Independence tab's period picker (this cycle, 2023–2026, 2021–2026 and so on, or one earlier cycle alone) adds up each cycle's totals (`campaign-history`, now with itemized, unitemized, transfers and self-funding), gift sizes, PAC lines, earmarks and joint fundraising. A cycle missing a record leaves the score waiting.
- **Campaign money by cycle:** the FEC's totals for the campaign committee in each two-year cycle back to 2016 (one request covers every cycle): raised, from individuals, from PACs, spent and cash at the end.
- **Campaign committee:** for a candidate whose principal committee is not configured, the FEC candidate-committee lookup, rechecked weekly.
- **PAC contributions:** processed FEC Form 3, Schedule A line 11C, in the selected two-year period. Memo entries are excluded. These contributions are part of campaign receipts.
- **Outside spending:** processed Schedule E with `most_recent=true`. Only general-election entries directly naming the candidate, with dissemination dates within the window, enter the current totals. Support and opposition are separate; opposition to another candidate is not credited to this person. Negative corrections remain. Exact repeated transactions are deduplicated; different transaction IDs remain distinct. Primary/special/unspecified elections, memo entries, future dates and missing dates are separately counted as exclusions.
- **Groups behind outside spending:** for the ten largest spenders in each race, their own filings from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer when the spender is a nonprofit with exactly that name. Anything else (super PACs, 527 groups, several possible matches) is listed for review with a link to the IRS's 527 filings search; the IRS publishes 527 data only as a bulk download.
- **Lobbying:** LDA.gov filings for each of the top 20 donor organizations in each year back to 2016 (up to 25 a year for each), with the bills each report names. Each week, for each key bill, the reports in its Congress that name it: how many, and how many of those read were filed for unions (by the client's name). These show beside the People First bill scores, never in them, since a report doesn't say which side the client was on. The bill number is searched in quotes, both with and without the space ("H.R. 1" and "H.R.1"), since LDA.gov matches loose words otherwise; a year's count is the larger of the two, shown as "at least" when both spellings find reports. The search is checked: if most reports returned don't name the bill, the job stops with an error and counts nothing. Foreign-agent registrations (FARA) are matched to the top donor organizations by name.
- **Federal contracts:** USAspending contracts to the top donor organizations from fiscal year 2016 on (the ten largest each), matched by the company's federal ID (UEI) from SAM.gov. Only a single exact name match is used; anything else is left for review. SAM.gov allows about 10 requests a day on a free key, so each organization is looked up once and the answer shared by everyone whose donors include it for 30 days; the check runs monthly, or at once for the new names when a person's top donors change, and a refusal waits six hours.
- **Net worth:** each sitting member's (and each profiled House candidate's, from the candidate report in the same House Clerk index) annual financial disclosures, every year since 2016 (for each year, an amendment over the original), so it can be followed over time, read weekly: House reports are PDFs listed in the House Clerk's disclosure index, each read once (a later run reads only a new year's report or an amendment); senators' are Senate eFD pages (report type 7, in the same agreed session as trades). Each asset's value and each debt's amount is a range, so the result is a range: lowest = assets at the bottom of their ranges minus debts at the top of theirs; highest = the reverse, open-ended ("or more") when an asset is "Over $50,000,000" or "Spouse/DC Over $1,000,000". Their home and a mortgage on it aren't on the form unless it earns income. A report filed on paper is listed to check by hand. The newest report is kept in full (its assets and debts); every year is kept in its `history` (one entry a year, newest first: its range, its individual stocks, and each earlier year's own asset and debt lists), and a new year adds to it, by the refresh or by hand. A year saved before the asset lists were kept is read again for its list (House; senators' through `npm run efd:fetch`). The Big Money Independence tab shows the assets one report at a time, with a year picker and a breakdown by kind (the middle of each value range, added up). Shown on the Ethics tab and the Overview's ethics card, with "Net worth by year" when more than one year is on file (`public/net-worth.js`, `public/holdings.js`), and on the Rankings page with the year before beside each person; no grade uses it. The Big Money Independence tab also draws it over time (`public/wealth-timeline.js`, charts in `public/charts.js`): net worth by year, each report's range from lowest to highest (a report filed on paper is an empty dashed box; an open-ended top fades out); beside it, the value of their individual stocks each year (left axis) with how much they bought and sold that year (right axis, added up from the middle of each trade's range); and underneath, every trade on the day it was made, buys above the line and sells below, bigger dots for bigger amounts and a ring for one reported late (after 45 days) or never. It uses the reviewed trades when there are any, otherwise those read from the official reports, and shows for a senator once their reports are imported from eFD.
- **Stock trades:** read straight from the members' own trade reports, with no key. For House members, each periodic transaction report listed in the House Clerk's yearly disclosure indexes (each year back to 2016; members are matched by state, last name and first name, with legal first names such as Michael for Mike, and `nicknames` in `data/config.json` for one no table would guess, like Garland for Andy Barr) is downloaded and its text read (`src/pdf.mjs`, which skips the bytes of pictures drawn inside a page, stops at a damaged string rather than reading the page again, and inflates at most 16 MB a stream and 64 MB a report, so a long scanned report can't run the local server out of memory); for senators, the Senate eFD site is searched for reports filed since January 2016 and each electronic report's table read. A report filed on paper is a scanned image, so it is listed under "Reports to check by hand" with its link. Reports already read are not downloaded again. A backlog (the years back to 2016, the first time) is read 80 reports at a time, about ten minutes apart. **When Senate eFD turns scripts away (HTTP 403),** its checks wait a week and say so; fetch the senators' report pages with a browser (`npm run efd:fetch`, below) or save them by hand, and import them with `npm run efd:import`, and they show like any other official report. Bargo (the last 3 months) runs alongside as a cross-check: the same trade from more than one place is merged into one row (same member, company, date, buy or sell, and amount range), the official report's date, description and link win, and a trade only a service reports is flagged for review. Each company's industry code comes from SEC EDGAR for the committee-conflict rule. Both sites' reports fall under the Ethics in Government Act, which bars using them for any commercial purpose other than news media, for credit ratings, or to solicit money; the Senate site asks every visitor to agree to that, and the reader agrees each session.
- **Comments on federal rules:** Regulations.gov comments by the top donor organizations (the newest 250 for each), as context for the Money & Influence Analysis.

**Record**

- **Legislation:** bills and resolutions each member sponsored or cosponsored from the 114th Congress (2015–16) on, from Congress.gov, with official links. Cosponsored bills are read in full weekly and, in between, only the bills updated since the last run. Profiles show the newest of each year, with counts by Congress; the snapshot lists every bill, for matching CRS reports and lobbying.
- **Bill details:** for each key bill (the bills behind the selected votes and the People First bills, listed as `keyBills` in `data/config.json`), its Congress.gov title, latest action, CBO cost estimates, committee reports, latest summary and amendments.
- **CRS reports:** Congressional Research Service reports from 2016 on, each read once and matched to the bills it covers (the first run catches up over several runs a few minutes apart; then a weekly run lists only reports updated since), shown on the profiles of people who sponsored or cosponsored them.
- **Official acts:** Federal Register documents (executive orders, rules) for profiled people who held federal executive office, set with `federalRegister` on the person in `data/config.json` (set for Donald Trump, `{"president":"donald-trump"}`).
- **Votes:** the 60 selected roll calls (the original 50, plus votes added for People First: the Cornyn prevailing-wage amendment to the CHIPS funding, the $15 minimum wage, the Paycheck Fairness Act in both chambers, the PRO Act's 2020 and 2021 House passage, the 2017 tax law's final Senate and House votes, and the 2019 House votes on the Raise the Wage Act and the Paycheck Fairness Act), the latest 30 Senate roll calls discovered from the official index, and new House roll calls. House votes from 2023 on come from Congress.gov's House vote endpoint when the Congress.gov key is set, and otherwise (and for votes it does not cover yet) from the House Clerk's files. This is not a complete historical voting archive. House positions use Bioguide IDs; Senate positions use LIS IDs from congress-legislators. When Senate.gov turns scripts away (HTTP 403), Senate roll calls and the list of new ones come from Voteview's free files instead (`src/voteview.mjs`), matched by Bioguide ID and kept only when the party totals add up to Voteview's own yea and nay counts; for six hours after a refusal, Senate votes go straight to Voteview. Those votes say "from Voteview" and link to its page. An unmatched person produces no inferred vote.
- **State legislative records:** bills, votes and sponsorships for profiled people who serve or served in a state legislature, from Open States (daily) and LegiScan (weekly) together, merged by state, session year and bill number. Each person is found by name in their state: Open States by their full name, then without a middle initial, and for a former legislator (not in its people search) through the bills they sponsored; LegiScan in the sessions of the years they served (`stateLegYears` in `data/config.json`, from the research's own entries). The ID found is kept with the snapshot, so it isn't searched for again. A career that ended before 2010, when the two services' coverage starts, shows "not covered" instead of a failed check. When more than one person matches, or none, the job stops and asks for the ID (`openstates` or `legiscan` on the person in `data/config.json`). Disagreements are listed for review against the legislature's own record.
- **Committee assignments:** the official Senate and House member lists, compared with the reviewed committee list and flagged where they differ.

**Statements**

- **Floor speeches:** the Congressional Record from GovInfo, matched to members by Bioguide ID. Each new daily issue is read once, speech by speech; every earlier issue back to 2016 is read once from its metadata file (one request a day of the Record). Profiles list the newest 40 speeches of each year, with every year's count.
- **Hearings:** GovInfo hearing transcripts that list a profiled member, published from 2016 on, and every committee meeting of the current Congress for the members' committees from Congress.gov's committee-meeting endpoint (earlier Congresses' meetings are left out: they're matched to members by committee seat, and only current seats are on file), with witnesses, witness testimony and committee documents (the House Document Repository's files, served through Congress.gov). Meetings are rechecked weekly as documents are added.
- **Videos:** uploads on each official's YouTube channel (from congress-legislators) back to 2016 (up to 1,000), read from the channel's uploads list at 1 quota unit a request: in full about once a month, and only the newest 50 on other days.
- **Issue positions:** Vote Smart interest-group ratings and the Political Courage Test, one person at a time, only with a paid Vote Smart license (off while the key is blank).
- **Press releases:** each sitting member's press releases, from the RSS or Atom feed on their official website. The job uses the feed the homepage advertises, then the feed congress-legislators lists, then the usual Senate and House feed addresses. They often carry the same message as the office's posts on X and Facebook, which have no free API.
- **Bluesky posts:** each person's official and campaign website domains are checked as Bluesky handles (only a domain's owner can use one), plus any accounts in `data/config.json` (`social.bluesky`). Run `npm run social:bluesky` on your computer to find more: it keeps only accounts linked from the person's official or campaign website, or carrying Bluesky's verified badge with their name, and `-- --write` saves them.
- **Threads posts:** each person's Threads account (`social.threads`, or else their Instagram username, which a Threads profile always uses) is searched for each issue's keyword, so only posts that mention an issue are collected. Needs `THREADS_ACCESS_TOKEN`.
- **Campaign websites:** every distinct Wayback Machine capture of each candidate's campaign site since 2016, read a year at a time (an ended year once), so a promise can be compared with earlier versions after it is edited or deleted.
- **Fact-checks:** published fact-checks of each politician's claims since 2016 (up to 200), from Google's Fact Check Tools, as leads for statement checks.
- **News coverage:** articles naming each politician, from GDELT (the last three months, the most its API serves) and the New York Times (the newest 10 of each year back to 2016) together, merged by link, as leads for statements and ethics cases.

**Ethics leads**

- **Court records:** CourtListener dockets and opinions naming profiled people (exact full-name search), newest first (up to 60 of each, with no start date), checked weekly to stay within the free account's daily limit.
- **FEC enforcement:** enforcement cases (MURs), administrative fines, alternative dispute resolutions and advisory opinions naming the person, their campaign committee, or a committee named after them.
- **DOJ press releases:** releases whose title names the person's surname and whose text names them in full, back to 2016 (up to 500 checked).

**How far back the refresh reads.** `historyFrom` in `data/config.json` (2016) sets where history starts. A year or election cycle that has ended is read once and kept, so each day only the open ones are read again; newest-first lists are read back once a month and their newest page daily. The first pass back to 2016 takes a few days on the local server (the New York Times allows 500 searches a day, and LDA.gov is read at 10 requests a minute); the Updates screen shows each source catching up.

**Sources that cover the same thing work together** (`src/merge.mjs`): each record shows once, whichever source reported it first, and each source fills in what the others don't have yet. Each joined card lists every source behind it with its status.

| What | Sources | How they're joined |
|---|---|---|
| Members' stock trades | House Clerk and Senate eFD reports (Senate ones imported by hand when eFD refuses), Bargo | Same member, company, date, type and amount. The official report's date and link win; a trade only a service has is kept and flagged until the official report is read; disagreements go to review. |
| House roll calls | Congress.gov, House Clerk | Congress.gov first (2023 on), the House Clerk for any vote it doesn't have. |
| State bills | Open States, LegiScan | Same state, session year and bill number. A bill only LegiScan has is kept. |
| News | GDELT, New York Times | Same article address. |
| Hearings | Congress.gov committee meetings, GovInfo transcripts | The transcript's jacket number, which the meeting lists; else the same title on the day it was held. The meeting adds witnesses, documents and bills; the transcript adds the text and who attended. |
| Legal leads | CourtListener, FEC enforcement records, DOJ press releases | A docket and an opinion in the same court with the same docket number are one case, and a DOJ release that cites the docket number joins it. Nothing is joined on a name or date alone. |
| Statements | Press releases, Bluesky, Threads, YouTube | A post that links to the press release, or repeats most of its words within 3 days. The press release leads. |
| Top donor organizations | FEC (employees' gifts), LDA.gov, FARA, Regulations.gov, SAM.gov, USAspending | One row per organization with what each source found for it. A source that hasn't run yet shows "Not checked yet", not "none". |
| Committee assignments | Official Senate and House lists, congress-legislators | Compared, and differences flagged. The saved list changes when `scripts/build-ids.mjs` is rerun. |

**Nothing new changes a grade by itself.** New votes, bills, speeches, posts, positions, fact-checks, news, court and enforcement records, trades and filings are listed for review; a grade changes only when the reviewed research is updated and the monthly recalculation runs. An ethics lead becomes a scored case only after its status is confirmed from an official record.

With every senator included there are about 3,300 checks, so the first full pass takes a few hours on the local server. When people who vote are added, the Senate or House roll calls already read are read once more so their positions are filled in. Each source is scheduled again 24 hours after completion (7 days for court records, FEC enforcement records, LegiScan, FARA, committee meetings and the campaign-committee lookup), except individual roll calls and Congressional Record issues: a published vote or Record issue is final and is not fetched again. Jobs that depend on another (the lobbying, rule-comment, company-ID and monthly-donor jobs wait for a person's top donor organizations; contracts wait for company IDs; nonprofit filings wait for the outside spenders) start as soon as it finishes. All pages must succeed before new records publish. Full replacements pick up revised totals, amendments and removed records instead of appending forever. Failed runs leave the last published snapshot intact and retry with backoff; source `Retry-After` is respected, and a rate limit pauses every job on that host. A database lock prevents overlapping refreshers. The UI provides on-page failure/staleness notices and a refresh history; external email or push alerts are not configured. An existing database gains the jobs for new sources the next time the server starts.

Some snapshots can have different check and coverage dates. In particular, checking the FEC today does not create a newer campaign filing. Imported comparisons stay labeled as research snapshots; refreshed records appear separately, under **Latest official records** at the bottom of each profile's tabs, so different reporting windows are not silently mixed:

- **Money & Influence:** campaign totals, PAC contributions, outside spending and the nonprofits behind it, top donor organizations, lobbying, foreign-agent registrations, comments on federal rules, company IDs and federal contracts
- **Legislative Activity → Your match:** sponsored and cosponsored bills, CRS reports, official acts, state legislative records, official committee lists and roll calls
- **Accountability → Promises & quotes:** floor speeches, hearings, committee meetings and testimony, press releases, Vote Smart positions and ratings, Bluesky and Threads posts, videos, campaign-site snapshots, fact-checks and news
- **Accountability → Ethics & personal finances:** net worth from the latest annual disclosure (a range), with a table of every year on file (its range, its individual stocks and the report), stock trades (with the filing-delay and sector charts), the official House disclosure filings, court records, FEC enforcement records and DOJ press releases

## Bundled snapshot

`data/seed.json`, when present, holds published snapshots from a completed refresh, so a new install shows campaign, PAC, outside-spending, legislation and vote data immediately instead of waiting hours, and works without API keys. **None has been committed yet**, so a new install currently starts empty and fills as the refresh runs. The file is loaded only when `.local/records.sqlite` is new; an existing database is never overwritten. Seeded records keep their original check dates, so with API keys an old seed refreshes in the background on the normal schedule. A seed made for a different cycle, Congress or session is ignored.

To update it, let the local server finish its refresh (every job on **API updates** and **Website updates** has completed at least once), then run `npm run seed:export` and commit `data/seed.json`. The export stops if any job has not completed yet (`npm run seed:export -- --partial` exports what has), and refuses to write a file containing any API key from `.dev.vars` or a link carrying a key.

## Scoring rules

The full rules, formulas and current-versus-preview status are in [Scores/00_POLITICAL_SCORECARD_METHODOLOGY.md](Scores/00_POLITICAL_SCORECARD_METHODOLOGY.md); the Methodology page in the app is the user-facing version. Published grade formulas live in `public/grades.js`, Integrity preview formulas in `public/integrity.js`, and `public/scoring.js` assembles a person's published scores. The individual score guides in `Scores/` explain each calculation and its limits.

**Rule 1: actions count more than words.** Sponsoring or cosponsoring a bill, voting on it, and an official act (for officials who don't vote in Congress) each weigh 1.0. A floor speech or public statement weighs 0.5 and is used only on an issue with no recorded action; once a vote, sponsorship or official act exists, it replaces earlier statements. Sponsoring a bill and then voting on it counts once (the vote is the one counted). Every bill has one ID, such as `117-hr-1319`, so a vote and a sponsorship can be matched. A Yea or a sponsorship takes the bill's marked side and a Nay the other; missed votes are not counted; if someone says one thing and votes the other way, only the vote is scored. A person who never held federal office but held a state office is scored on that state record, with the same weights.

| Grade | What it measures | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Your Match** | How closely does this politician's record align with your views? It compares the visitor's issue positions with documented positions and recorded actions, weighted by the importance they gave each issue, and is personalized to their answers. A higher score means closer agreement; it does not measure honesty, ethics, or whether policies benefit working people. Issues with no evidence are left out; statement-only issues count at half importance | 3 issues with evidence, covering half of the visitor's total importance |
| **Integrity** (provisional) | How consistently does this politician keep commitments, manage financial conflicts, and meet accountability standards? Honesty (30%, the Promises Delivered Index), Personal Finances (30%: the Insider Trading and Stock Disclosure Dodger indexes plus holdings and trading penalties) and Fundraising (40%: donor sizes, PAC-type, PAC-reliance and earmark penalties and verified pledge adjustments, adapted from IntegrityIndex.us), weights normalized over complete components, then minus the full Legal Trouble Index, bounded 0–100. Parameters still need calibration | 2 complete components and a dated, sourced Legal Trouble review; Honesty needs 3 counted promise checks |
| **People First** | How consistently do this politician's legislative actions prioritize working people? 0.75 × legislative-action score + 0.25 × (100 − the existing Corporation Compromised Index); the legislative-action score alone when there is no index. Legislative-action score: Working-people points ÷ all points, from votes, sponsorships and cosponsorships on scored bills. Each bill is scored from −100 (fully corporate-backed) to +100 (fully worker-backed), and each action counts the bill's strength (score ÷ 100) for the side it took, at its Rule 1 weight. For a president, signing a marked bill or issuing a marked executive order (keyed `eo-14251`) counts for its side and a veto for the other side. A higher grade means their record works more for lower- and middle-income households | 3 scored actions |
| **Promises Delivered Index** (historical grade; now Integrity's Honesty) | Whether what they committed to matches what they did: supported 1, partly supported or changed position ½, contradicted 0. Ambiguous and outcome checks, promises with no related bill yet, vague goals and statements made in debate the day of the vote or the day before are left out. A promise they could have acted on but didn't by the end of the term counts as contradicted. For a president, PolitiFact's promise-tracker tallies (`promiseTrackers`: the Trump-O-Meter and MAGA-Meter for Donald Trump) count the same way: Promise Kept 1, Compromise ½, Promise Broken 0, with Stalled and In the Works left out | 3 counted checks |
| **Ethics** (historical grade; its indexes now feed Integrity) | 100 − Legal Trouble Index − a tenth of the Insider Trading Index − a tenth of the Stock Disclosure Dodger Index, rounded and never below 0. Legal Trouble: each case's status points (finding 20, open 15, closed 5, allegation 0) × kind (criminal 1.5, ethics or civil 1, technical 0.5) × age (0.5 when decided over 10 years ago), with a ceiling of 100. Insider Trading: committee conflicts, trades near related votes, trades in donors and single-stock activity. Stock Disclosure Dodger: late or missing trade reports and late annual disclosures *(placeholders)* | None: "none located" scores 100% |

Grades: A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69, F below 60, after rounding to a whole percentage. Too little evidence shows "Not graded", never a zero or a guess.

**Not graded:** the ideological position ("More conservative than 48% of the Senate", from Voteview's DW-NOMINATE, Congress only) and the key-vote position ("Conservative side on 4 of 9 marked bills"). The key-vote marking is `liberalSide` / `leanNote` on each issue in `public/research.json`; the logic is in `public/lean.js`.

## Marking and tagging the reviewed research

These judgments are made by people and stored in `public/research.json` (and two small files beside it), never by the refresher:

- **People First bill scores:** each bill is stored once in `bills` with its evidence, so a vote and a sponsorship on the same bill share it: `{"id": "117-hr-5376", "title": "...", "tax": true, "gains": {"step": 50, "by": "Tax Policy Center", "why": "...", "src": {"t": "...", "u": "..."}}, "sides": [{"group": "aflcio", "pos": "support", "date": "...", "why": "...", "src": {...}}], "context": {"cbo": {...}, "whiteHouse": {...}}}`. `gains.step` is one of +100, +50, 0, −50, −100 (the five steps in `GAINS_STEPS`, `public/grades.js`). `sides` lists positions from the groups in `BILL_GROUPS`: the AFL-CIO, the Economic Policy Institute and the National Employment Law Project on the worker side, and the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, NFIB and the National Association of Manufacturers on the business side, plus ITEP and the Tax Foundation on bills marked `tax`. `billScore` averages the two parts, sets a bill aside as Disputed when they point opposite ways, and needs opposite sides when groups' positions are all there is. `context` is shown, never scored. 14 bills and amendments are on file and 10 are scored. `access` (same steps, `ACCESS_STEPS`) records a cited estimate of a change in access to a basic need, and is averaged with `gains`. `versions` lists the same bill's numbers in other Congresses, so it counts once per person. `stated` holds cited public statements (`{person, pos: support|oppose, date, why, src}`), which count half a vote. Bills that didn't pass count through cosponsorships and statements; the infrastructure law and the CHIPS and Science Act are listed as Even (both sides supported them). An amendment is keyed on its own (`117-samdt-1858`), so its vote isn't merged with the bill's. North America's Building Trades Unions and Associated Builders and Contractors count only on measures marked `construction`, the way ITEP and the Tax Foundation count only on `tax` bills. After adding bills, rerun `node scripts/build-ids.mjs` so the new ones get bill-detail and lobbying jobs.
- **The bill list (Bills page):** `billIndex`, one entry per bill family: `{"id": "117-hr-8404", "title": "...", "status": "law", "law": "Public Law 117-228", "date": "2022-12-13", "versions": [...], "groups": [{"group": "heritage", "pos": "oppose", "why": "...", "src": {...}, "date": "..."}]}`, or for a bill that isn't law `"status": "not"` with `"stage"` (how far it got) and `"reintroduced": true`. A bill with no saved roll call can carry `summary`, `summarySrc` and `steps` (`[{"text": "...", "src": {...}}]`). Group keys are those in `groupPos`, the People First groups, or `aclu` (`public/bills.js`). Every bill behind a saved roll call must be listed (the tests check). A bill with `"issue"`, `"side"` and `"why"` counts in Your match: each profiled sponsor or cosponsor (its `cosponsors`, filled by `npm run bills:cosponsors` along with the People First bills) gets one action on that issue toward `side` (`issueEvidence` in `public/bills.js`). The six anti-corruption bills (stock-trading bans, the lifetime lobbying ban and the Democracy for All Amendment) count this way under "Money in politics and congressional ethics". The groups' positions on each saved roll call (`groupPos`, below) are added to the bills they were cast on.
- **PAC types from the FEC:** `pacIds` (parallel to `pacNames`: each PAC's FEC committee ID, or null) and `pacCommittees` (`{"C00000422": [name, committee type, designation, organization type]}`), written by `npm run pacs:types` (`scripts/build-pac-types.mjs`, needs `FEC_API_KEY`). It matches each saved PAC name to the contributor IDs on the FEC's processed PAC contributions for that person's committee (a name cut off at 40 characters matches the full name it starts; a name that could be two committees stays unmatched), looks up the names still unmatched in the FEC's committee names (one exact match only), then reads each committee's registration. `public/pac-types.js` turns the FEC codes into kinds: corporate, trade association, membership group, cooperative, labor union, lobbyist, leadership, nonconnected, candidate, party, joint fundraising. The sponsor's type decides first, so a company's PAC stays corporate when it is also registered as a lobbyist PAC. Shown next to the sector this app assigns, in the PAC table, a by-type chart and each PAC's hover description. Shown only; no grade uses it yet. Rerun it after new PAC rows are added; `--check` lists what it finds without writing.
- **Election results:** `election` (`{"date": "2026-11-03", "asof": "2026-11-13", "note": "..."}`) and a `result` on each race in `races` and `liteRaces`: `{"winner": "ossoff", "date": "2026-11-04", "how": "Called by the AP", "src": {"t": "...", "u": "..."}}`, where "date" is the day of the call or of the count on file (the ticker dates each call by it) and the winner is a person ID in a profiled race and an FEC candidate ID in a toss-up race. A race no news organization has called and nobody has conceded has `"winner": null`, an optional `"leader"`, and `how` saying where the count stands; nobody is shown as having won or lost until it has a winner. Ohio's carries `"special": true` (its winner takes the seat once the state certifies). Each result also has a `tally`: `{"asof": "2026-11-04", "shares": {"ossoff": 59.0, ...}, "votes"?: {...}, "others"?: [{"name", "party"?, "pct", "votes"?}], "total"?: <votes counted>, "counted"?: "91% of the expected vote counted", "src": {...}}`, one news report or results page per race, with each candidate's share as reported. `public/results.js` turns these into the Races page's tiles (photo, name, party and share, plus the reported turnout: the source's total, or the sum of all candidates' votes when every one was given) and the Won, Lost and Not called yet labels on home cards and profiles. Results-only races (`liteRaces`, with `chamber` "S" or "H": the other 14 Senate races and 14 House toss-ups) list `nominees`, each known by `fecCand` or, without one, `id` (a sitting senator's directory ID, so their tile links to their Senate profile, or "state-lastname"). Toss-up nominees' photos are keyed by their FEC candidate ID (`bioguide` on a nominee gives an official portrait; `scripts/portrait-extra.json` takes a supplied one). To record a call later, set the race's `winner`, `how` and `src`, remove `leader`, and update `election.asof`. No grade uses them.
- **Winners of results-only races:** a winner of a race in `liteRaces` who wasn't already in Congress gets a full profile in `cands`, marked `"resultsOnly": true`; the race's nominee names it (`"profile": "colom"`), so the race tile, the ticker and the profile find each other (a nominee is known by `"id"` when it has one, else by its FEC candidate ID). Their campaign money is the principal committee's own totals from the FEC's committee summary file (`npm run money:update` keeps it current), and their outside spending is the race's items in `ie.rows`, general election through Sept. 30, 2026 like every profile; what they told voters is tagged in `data/question-statements.json`.
- **Money pledges:** a statement tagged `"pledge": {"kind": "corporate-pac" | "single-issue-pac" | "stock-trading" | "lobbying" | "citizens-united", "since"?, "refunded"?}` is checked against the records on file by `public/pledges.js` and shown under its reviewed verdict on the Promises tab; it never changes the Promise score. The bills it reads are `billIndex` entries tagged `"reform"` (with `"versions"` for earlier Congresses).
- **Holdings status (step 8):** `"holdings": {"status": "blind-trust" | "no-stocks", "since", "src", "also"?, "note"}` on a person. A qualified blind trust takes reviewed trades marked `"inTrust": true` out of both trading indexes (`ownTrades` in `public/grades.js`); "no-stocks" labels the Insider Trading Index. `public/holdings.js` reads a lead from the latest annual report's assets.
- **Corporation Compromised Index (step 9):** computed, not stored: `corporationCompromised` and `workersOverProfit` in `public/grades.js`, fed by `makeScorer(data, committees, sectors, tallies)` in `public/scoring.js` from each person's PAC rows, `camp` totals, `funderPos` and the roll calls' party totals (`public/vote-party.json`). Business funders are listed in `BUSINESS_FUNDERS` (`public/influence.js`).
- **Funder groups and their positions:** `groupPos`, one entry per group whose money and public positions are compared on profiles (36 today, from the Chamber of Commerce, AFL-CIO and Heritage Action to the crypto industry's Fairshake PACs, EMILY's List, AIPAC, J Street, the hospital, doctor, bank, credit-union, realtor and drug-company associations, the teachers' unions and the gun and abortion groups): `{"key": "nea", "org": "...", "committees": [...], "fecIds": [{"id": "C00003251", "name": "..."}], "pacMatch": ["^National Education Association"], "positions": [{"voteId": "h-119-1-145", "wanted": "Nay", "basis": "statement", "scoredAs": "...", "src": {...}}], "status": "done", "notFound": "..."}`. `basis` is `scorecard`, `key_vote_alert` (a letter before the vote) or `statement`. A group's PAC gifts are matched by the names in `committees` and `fecIds` (FEC names are cut off at about 40 characters, so a cut-off name matches) or by the `pacMatch` patterns; its outside spending by `fecIds`. `notFound` says what was looked for and not found. `conduits` lists the committee IDs of the PACs through which a group bundles its donors' gifts (AIPAC, Club for Growth, EMILY's List, J Street, the Senate Conservatives Fund). `npm run funders:bundled` (`scripts/build-bundled.mjs`, needs `FEC_API_KEY`) reads what each of those PACs forwarded to each profiled campaign in the cycles its PAC reports cover, from the PAC's own disbursements (a line saying "earmark" is a forwarded gift; a total line counts once, not again with the gifts listed under it; the PAC's own checks stay in the PAC rows), and writes `bundled` (`{asOf, conduits, names, discovered, cycles, people: {person: {group: {routed, n, direct, cycles, byCycle, src}}}}`). It also finds every other committee forwarding gifts: the FEC's totals by recipient (`schedules/schedule_b/by_recipient_id`) list every committee that sent the campaign money in each cycle, and any that sent more than a PAC may give directly ($10,000 a cycle), many checks, or memo entries has its disbursements read the same way (saved as `fec:C…`, with its name in `names`). Each cycle from 2016 on is kept in `byCycle`; `routed` stays the current cycle's. A committee's disbursements are read only for the cycles it gave in. `listed` marks each person searched completely (every cycle's totals by recipient and every group's gifts read in full), so their Big Money Independence Earmarks part isn't marked partial; `discovered: true` means everyone was. A search that stops at one of its limits (`LIMITS` in the script: 500 committees in a cycle's list of who gave, 20,000 payments from one committee in a cycle, and the 10 largest other possible conduits from each list) leaves the person partial too: the run lists what each limit cut short, the earlier saved total is kept, and with none saved, the total read so far is kept marked `"partial": true`. When the last limit is the one that stopped a person, the run's "To finish them" line adds `--max-found` with a higher number, to read more of that person's possible conduits in one run. The end of a run names anyone not searched completely and the `--only` command that finishes them; a run with `--only` keeps everyone else's results. `-- --only ossoff,collins` reads just those people. For a file saved before `listed` was kept, `-- --mark-complete-except a,b` marks everyone else complete with no FEC requests (once; take the ids from the run's list of failed reads). It waits out the FEC key's hourly limit. ActBlue, WinRed and other platforms that forward gifts for any campaign aren't conduits here. After changing a group, its positions, the saved votes or the FEC rows, run `node scripts/build-funders.mjs` (`--check` lists the changes without writing): it rewrites each person's `funderPos` (what each group gave, bundled as `routed` or spent, and its positions next to the person's votes). The tests check the saved copy matches a rebuild.
- **Bills marked liberal or conservative:** `liberalSide` / `leanNote` on each issue.
- **Promises:** a statement check that is a true commitment carries `"promise": true`; a vague goal ("fight for families") carries `"vague": true` and is listed but not scored, as is a statement made in debate the day of the vote or the day before (`"debate": true`); a check the promise finder found carries `"found"` (its source, how it was linked and the review date); a promise with nothing to act on yet has `"assessment": "pending"`. A promise left unacted at the end of the term is closed as `"assessment": "contradiction"` with `"unacted": {"date": "...", "reason": "..."}`. A changed-position check can carry `"issue"` (an issue ID) so the position-change timing flag can link it to donors' positions on that issue.
- **Ethics cases:** `status` (allegation, investigation or finding) and, for investigations, `stage` (open or closed). When a case changes, its status is updated rather than a new entry added, so it counts once. Leads from court records, FEC enforcement records, DOJ press releases and news become cases only after a reviewer confirms their status from an official record.
- **Stock trades:** reviewed trades go in the person's `trades`: `{"traded": "2026-01-05", "filed": "2026-03-02", "company": "...", "ticker": "...", "sector": "...", "asset": "stock", "type": "purchase", "amount": "$1,001 - $15,000", "owner": "spouse", "src": {"t": "...", "u": "..."}}`, with `"missing": true` for a trade never disclosed and `"flag": "..."` for one flagged for review (not scored). Sectors are those in `public/sectors.js`, assigned from the company's SEC industry code.
- **Committee sectors:** which industries each committee oversees, for committee-conflict trades, in `public/committee-sectors.json` (a draft to review). Committee seats themselves are in `public/committees.json`, written by `scripts/build-ids.mjs`.

Who assigns People/Profit and liberal/conservative marks, and how disagreements are settled, is an open decision in the methodology.

## Update process

The data underneath refreshes daily (see What refreshes). Scores are recalculated once a month, after review, matching the monthly donation data:

1. Review everything new since the last update on **API updates**, **Website updates** and each profile's Latest official records: votes, sponsorships, floor speeches, statements and campaign-site changes, fact-checks, ethics leads (court records, FEC enforcement records, DOJ press releases, news) and case changes, stock-trade disclosures (and `data/holdings-review.json`), donation and lobbying filings.
2. Add new People First bills with their cited evidence: who gains, and each group's position.
3. Match new actions to existing promises, and replace earlier statements with votes where Rule 1 applies. `npm run promises:find` reads the collected statements for commitments linked to bills they then voted on (data/promise-queue.json); `npm run campaign:collect` reads each profiled person's campaign website as it stood just before each election day since 2016 (the Wayback Machine's saved copies of the homepage and the pages about where they stand) and their campaign-season Bluesky and Threads posts, as text, into data/campaign-text/, the first step toward checking campaign promises against the term that followed. The Wayback Machine is often slow: each request gets 30 seconds and two more tries, a site search that fails isn't asked again for that site in the same run, a campaign keeps the pages it did get, and five failed requests in a row stop the run (the Wayback Machine is down). Each person is saved as soon as they're done, so it can be stopped with Ctrl+C at any time; running it again reads only the campaigns that failed or left pages unread (`-- --redo` reads them all). `npm run promises:review` goes through them one at a time and adds the ones you confirm to the person's record (see the [Honesty section](Scores/03_INTEGRITY_SCORE.md#honesty-the-promises-delivered-index-)).
4. Run `npm run scores:monthly` to recalculate Keeps Their Word, Shows Up and People First for everyone graded (the reviewed profiles and every senator's and House member's basic profile) and rerun the Money & Influence timing flags (`-- --dry-run` previews it). It uses the page's own code and files (`gradedPeople`, `sixContext` and `makeScorer` in `public/scoring.js`), so a logged grade is the one the profile shows; a test checks both build their scores the same way. Big Money Independence, and Plays by the Rules once a profile has read the refresh's trade records, also use the refresh's saved records, so they aren't logged yet.
5. It adds an entry to `public/score-log.json`: which grade moved, by how much and why, compared with the last entry of the same formula. Entries from before November 10, 2026 logged the grades the six scores replaced (Promises delivered, Ethics and an earlier People First formula); they're kept as they were, marked `"formula": "retired"`. The Methodology page shows the latest entries.
6. `npm run integrity:monthly` writes `public/integrity-preview-log.json`, separate from official historical grade logs. Integrity is shown as a primary interface grade from the comparison snapshot, but its methodology is still provisional and uncalibrated; it never rewrites historic Ethics/Promise scores or People First. The adapted structure credits [IntegrityIndex.us](https://integrityindex.us/methodology).

## Money & Influence Analysis

Shown beside the grades, never inside them. It shows timing and alignment, not proof that money caused a decision. The rules are in `public/influence.js`; the thresholds (top 20 donors, a 50% change, a 6-month window) are placeholders under review.

- **Vote Donor Alignment:** of the votes on bills a group that gave to the campaign (its PAC's checks or bundled gifts) took a public position on (its scorecard or key-vote letter, from the reviewed funder research), the share that went the group's way. Missed votes are not counted.
- **Speech Donor Alignment:** not measured yet. Floor speeches arrive from the Congressional Record and need to be coded by issue and side before they can be compared.
- **Donations by month:** each researched group's PAC contributions to the campaign month by month since January 2015, the start of the 2016 cycle (FEC bulk data), with the votes it took a position on marked under its row. From the daily FEC refresh, the top 20 donor organizations in the current cycle combine each organization's PAC with its employees' itemized contributions, grouped by employer (donors' names are never shown), with their employees' giving by month.
- **Timing flags** (for review, not scored), within a 6-month window: donations from a group rose 50% or more from a vote it wanted, compared with the 6 months before; fell 50% or more after a vote it opposed; the person changed position on an issue after a 50% shift in donations from a group that took positions on it; or the person held or traded stock in a group that donated and took positions on bills they acted on. A window that runs past the data is not judged. Each flag lists the group, the amounts before and after, the decision and the dates.
- **Stock holdings and trades** (in the analysis, for review): a "Stock in donors" figure and a section listing reviewed trades in a donor's company, shown as diamonds on that donor's row of the monthly chart; and, from the daily refresh, the latest disclosed trades (not yet reviewed) checked against the top 20 donor organizations, flagged when that organization also lobbied on a bill the person acted on. Trades are covered for members of Congress (Periodic Transaction Reports); a president's OGE Form 278-T reports are not imported yet, and anyone else shows "No trading data". Late filings and committee-conflict trades count in Ethics, not here.
- **Who had a stake:** lobbying reports (LDA.gov) filed for top donor organizations that name a bill the person sponsored, cosponsored or voted on; foreign-agent registrations, comments on federal rules and federal contracts are listed as context.

Outside spending is shown as a plain number, not a grade: the politician cannot legally coordinate with those groups, and the amounts mostly reflect how competitive the race was. Outside spenders have no industry code, so their sector is assigned by this app from each committee's name (unions People-aligned, industry and corporate groups Profit-aligned, party, ideological and candidate-aligned groups Other), the same way PAC sectors are, and is under review.

## Score pages

- **Ideological position:** the whole chamber on the DW-NOMINATE scale, with the selected politician highlighted.
- **Political compass** (the top of every profile's Ideology tab, `public/compass.js`): a square with economic views across (left to right) and social views up and down (socially liberal at the bottom, conservative at the top). Under the title, in small capitals, "ECONOMIC: +38 right (4 issues) · SOCIAL: −25 liberal (4 issues)", each number blue to the left or down and red to the right or up (the Ideology tab's lean colors; within 20 of the middle reads center or moderate), the two on separate lines on a phone. The person's portrait sits at their place; everyone else in their chamber is a bubble by party (bigger = more members at the same spot, with the count on bubbles of 10 or more and under the portrait when others share theirs; a single member's bubble opens their profile's Ideology tab). Beside it, the position card and its sources; under it, the DW-NOMINATE chart, in smaller type, moved up from "Shown, not counted". The compass is drawn at the size it's shown (`mountCompass` in `public/app.js`), so its labels never shrink, and beside the cards it's sized so the left column ends where the right one does (at least 400 pixels); under 1000 pixels wide everything stacks: compass, DW-NOMINATE, position, sources.

## Alignment and ideology questions (being built)
My alignment and the Ideology tab are moving from the 16 coded issues to **250 questions in 17 categories** (`public/questions.json`): the topics of iSideWith.com's political quiz in our own wording, without its Trending questions, its local ones (city and state decisions) or its inflation question, with its duplicates merged, NAFTA as the USMCA, and 5 questions of ours for votes the quiz has no question for (union rights, the CHIPS Act, birth control, rejecting certified electoral votes, war powers). Each question has two sides, the index of its liberal side (or null when it splits both parties or has no left–right side: those count in My alignment only), and its compass axis (`econ` across, `social` up and down, or none). Ids never change once published; rewording keeps a question's evidence, and a change of meaning gets a new id.

How a politician's stance on a question will be worked out (the plan, approved):
- Points toward each side (formula questions-3: what they did weighs most): **1** per vote and **1** for cosponsoring, **1.5** for sponsoring a bill or an official act; **up to 3** from a group rating on that topic (3 × |2 × score − 100| ÷ 100, toward the group's side above 50%, the other side below; letter grades A 100, B 75, C 50, D 25, F 0); and per statement that takes a side (a press release, floor speech, campaign-site issues page, or the text of a post) **1** when they have votes, bills or official acts on the question, **3** only when they have none (challengers, and questions Congress never voted on), where the row reads "from statements only". One credit per bill (sponsoring and voting on the same bill counts once, at the higher value), and repeats count once: a bill reintroduced in a later Congress and (co)sponsored again counts once (its family is its title without the year, `familyOf` in `public/question-scores.js`), and votes on the same bill family in one two-year Congress count once; a vote on it in a later Congress counts again.
- **Group ratings on the questions** (`groups` in `public/questions.json`, worked out by `ratingItems` in `public/question-scores.js` from `public/group-ratings.json`): a group whose scorecard is about one topic counts on that topic's question, once. SBA Pro-Life America (Pro-life), Reproductive Freedom for All and Planned Parenthood Action (Pro-choice) on abortion; LCV on tightening climate rules (Yes); Gun Owners of America on more gun purchase limits (No); Club for Growth and Americans for Prosperity on cutting spending to reduce the debt (Yes); NumbersUSA on tighter border security (Yes); Stand With Crypto on stricter crypto regulation (No); AJP Action on continued support for Israel (No); NIAC Action on war with Iran (No). Groups that rate across many topics (Heritage Action, CPAC, the John Birch Society, the Republican Liberty Caucus, ProgressivePunch, FRC Action, the Humane World Action Fund, the Alliance for Retired Americans) and the Council for Innovation Promotion (patents, no question) count only in the Ratings lean. Each rating is listed in the question's record with its points.
- **Statements on the questions** (`data/question-statements.json`, checked and copied to `public/question-statements.json` by `npm run statements:record`): what each reviewed profile told voters, tagged by hand to a question with the side it takes, the quote, the date and a link. The sources are their campaign websites as the Wayback Machine saved them and their campaign-season Bluesky and Threads posts (`data/campaign-text/`), plus the reviewed statements in `public/research.json`. `npm run statements:find` lists each person's sentences that name a question's search phrases (`data/question-search.json` "any" and "said") in `.local/statement-candidates/` to review. A tag is made only when the words clearly take one side; a statement that only explains a vote isn't a separate position; at most one statement per question per campaign, and the same words on several pages count once, at the latest date. A reviewed statement already carried over on the same question with the same link counts once. Floor speeches, press releases and Bluesky and Threads posts collected by the local refresh (`.local/records.sqlite`) are exported for tagging by `npm run statements:export` (`scripts/export-statements.mjs`) on the computer that has the database: it writes `data/collected-statements/<id>.json` with only the sentences on a question's topic (floor speeches in the speaker's own words, read from GovInfo with the Congress.gov key, up to `--read` new ones a run; with `--wait` it saves what's read and pauses an hour whenever GovInfo says to slow down, then goes on), and `npm run statements:find` reads those files alongside the campaign text. A statement outside a campaign site belongs to the election its date leads up to (2025 counts with 2026).
- **Said it, then voted against it:** the statement is cancelled (it counts 0) and the vote counts as usual. Only a later vote on a bill clearly about the question does this (not a large package that happens to include it), and a statement that explains a vote isn't a separate position. **Voted one way, then later said the opposite:** the statement counts as their current position and the old vote still counts, so their stance can read Mixed ("Changed position").
- Stance = (points for the first side − points for the second) ÷ all points, −100 to +100, shown as Yes / Leans yes / Mixed / Leans no / No; under 3 points, "Not on record" (unknown is never no).
- My alignment: agreement on a question = (1 + your side × their stance) ÷ 2 (stance −1 to +1), weighted by the importance you give it (Least 1 to Most 5), averaged over the questions where you both have a stance; the same by category.
- Ideology: a question's lean is the stance with the conservative side positive; a category's lean is the average of its questions'; the overall lean the average of the categories' (at least 3). The compass uses each question's axis. The tabs keep their cards and charts; only what they say changes.
- Bills: every question is matched to as many bills as possible from GovInfo's BILLSTATUS bulk files (titles, subjects, policy areas, the Congressional Research Service's summaries, sponsors, cosponsors and recorded votes), each coded with the side it takes; roll calls from Voteview.

## Latest updates strip

Under the top bar, a strip scrolls the site's updates from the last 24 hours, newest first, each with a tag, its date and a link: race calls (from `research.json`'s `races`, within 24 hours of the results' `asof`), newly reported stock trades and annual financial disclosures from the refresh, the interest-group ratings read (`group-ratings.json`'s `checked`), and site updates. Older updates drop off. Races still counting stay on whatever their date, as long as the results are under 45 days old. A record dated by day alone counts from noon UTC that day. A site update appears only when its entry in `public/score-presentation-log.json` has a `headline`, the short line the strip shows; entries without one, and every rule change, stay in the log and on the methodology page only. The trades and disclosures come from `/api/ticker`: when the refresh publishes a House or Senate trades or disclosures check, it keeps that check's newest items (at most 10, from the last 14 days of trades and 30 of disclosures) in the metadata table as `ticker:<job id>`, each stamped `seen` with when a check first read it, and the endpoint merges them (at most 40). The 24 hours run from `seen`, so a report filed days ago but read today is today's news; items on a check's first list aren't stamped and go by their own date. With nothing to show, the strip isn't shown. It stops while hovered or focused, stands still for visitors whose device asks for reduced motion, and is left out when printing. The code is `public/ticker.js`.

## Portraits

Home cards show a cut-out portrait standing in a party-colored circle; anyone without a photo shows initials in the same circle. Photos are official congressional portraits (public domain) from the [unitedstates/images](https://github.com/unitedstates/images) project, for everyone profiled and every other sitting senator in `public/senate.json`, plus photos supplied for use here, kept in `data/portraits/` and listed with their credit in `scripts/portrait-extra.json` (these take precedence over the official one). For anyone still showing initials, `npm run portraits:fetch` (`scripts/fetch-portrait-photos.mjs`; `-- --dry-run` to only list, `-- --only <ids>`) looks for the lead photo of their Wikipedia article (the article must be titled with their name and name their state in its opening) and saves it when it is on Wikimedia Commons under a Creative Commons license that allows changes and commercial use, or in the public domain, listing its author, license and file page as the credit. `scripts/build-portraits.py` downloads the official photos, removes the background with MediaPipe, scales and places every face the same way, and writes `public/portraits/<id>.webp` plus `public/portraits/index.json` (who has a photo, its source and credit). To rebuild:

```
python3 -m venv .local/pv
.local/pv/bin/pip install mediapipe==0.10.14 pillow
.local/pv/bin/python scripts/build-portraits.py            # add --refresh to download the photos again
.local/pv/bin/python scripts/build-portraits.py --only osborn,alme   # rebuild just those people
```

A newly appointed senator may have no official portrait in unitedstates/images yet; their card keeps the initials until one is added there or a photo is supplied as described below (Alan Armstrong's and Darline Graham Nordone's were supplied in October 2026). If a cut-out comes out empty, the script retries with MediaPipe's other segmentation model and then the other photo size.

To add or replace a photo, save one you have permission to use as `data/portraits/<id>.jpg`, list it in `scripts/portrait-extra.json` with its credit, and rebuild: `"talarico": {"file": "data/portraits/talarico.jpg", "credit": "Photo: Talarico campaign, used with permission"}`. If a cut-out keeps a light halo from a bright background, add `"tighten": true` and `"choke": 2` to that entry (as for El-Sayed and Jackson) and rebuild. If something wider than the frame, like a hat brim, is cut off on one side, `"nudge": <px>` moves the portrait sideways, and `"zoom": 0.85` shrinks it when it is wider than the whole frame (as for Byrd). A photo that is already a transparent PNG cut-out keeps its own outline.

## Deploy later to Cloudflare

The local and Cloudflare servers share the same refresh and API implementation. There is no separate frontend build.

1. `npx wrangler login`
2. `npx wrangler d1 create public-ledger`
3. Put the returned database ID in `wrangler.jsonc`.
4. `npm run db:migrate`
5. `npx wrangler secret put FEC_API_KEY`, then the same for `CONGRESS_API_KEY`, `GOVINFO_API_KEY`, `OPENSTATES_API_KEY`, `LEGISCAN_API_KEY`, `LDA_API_KEY`, `BARGO_API_KEY`, `COURTLISTENER_API_TOKEN`, `GOOGLE_API_KEY`, `NYT_API_KEY`, `REGULATIONS_API_KEY`, `SAM_API_KEY` and `SEC_USER_AGENT` (skip any you don't use)
6. `npm run cloudflare:deploy`

The hosted cron checks every five minutes, processing up to eight source pages per invocation. With every source enabled there are about 1,000 jobs plus one per new Congressional Record issue, so the first full refresh takes most of a day. Use the Workers paid plan for the configured CPU allowance. D1 and Worker usage may incur charges beyond included allowances; this project does not enable billing or create any cloud resources by itself. The hosted database begins empty and fills from official sources; the reviewed snapshot is bundled with the static assets. Local SQLite files and API keys are excluded from deployment.

Deployment remains a later manual step because this version was requested to stay local.

## Choices behind this design

- **Every source that can run alongside the others is used.** Using several services for the same data adds merging work but gives cross-checks and keeps data flowing if one service changes its free tier or shuts down, as Lambda Finance did.
- **State records: Open States and LegiScan together,** merged by state bill number. LegiScan is checked weekly, which keeps it well inside its monthly query cap.
- **Speeches: the GovInfo API directly.** The refresher runs in JavaScript, so the `crec` Python package isn't needed.
- **Committee documents and testimony through Congress.gov's committee-meeting endpoint,** which serves the House Document Repository's files with structured witness data, rather than reading the repository's pages.
- **Stock trades: the official House and Senate reports, read directly,** with Bargo, Eulerpool and Financial Modeling Prep as a cross-check. Duplicate trades are merged, disagreements go to review, and lateness uses the official report's filing date. Capitol API is not used: it reads the same official filings.
- **Senate votes use LIS IDs** from congress-legislators, replacing surname-and-state matching.
- **Donor names stay hidden:** FEC rules restrict reusing individual contributors' information, so donors are grouped by employer and names are never kept.
- **House votes from Congress.gov** for 2023 on when its key is set, since it now serves them officially; the House Clerk's files remain for older votes and as the fallback.
- **Stock-trade history:** the refresh reads both chambers' official trade reports from 2016 on. Yearly holdings come from `scripts/build-holdings.py`, checked by hand.
- **Court records weekly, profiled people only:** CourtListener's free account allows about 125 requests a day.
- **News from GDELT and the New York Times together,** merged by link.
- **Overlapping sources are joined, not listed side by side:** hearings, legal leads, statements across channels and top donor organizations each show as one card fed by all their sources (see the table under What refreshes), so one source's gaps are filled by another's.
- **Grades are computed from the reviewed research and logged monthly.** The page always reflects the reviewed files, and the monthly log records the same grades with the same code, so every change between runs is listed with its reason.
- **Left out because they repeat work already done, not because of a key:** the `crec` package (the GovInfo API is used directly) and Capitol API (CapitolGains reads the same filings). Sources that are shut down, closing or paid are listed in [.01_API_SOURCES_USED.md](.01_API_SOURCES_USED.md).

# Public Ledger
### Follow the money. Check the record. Find your alignment.

<img width="200" height="200" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fa265870-665c-41b5-8ec7-2bce1c1c7391" />

Public Ledger helps voters understand **who funds their elected officials, how they vote, and whether their actions match their promises**.

The platform brings complex public records into one easy-to-read **candidate scorecard**, helping users explore:

- **Campaign Funding:** Who financially supports a candidate and how funding patterns relate to their legislative record.
- **Legislative Record:** Bills introduced, sponsored, and voted on, with plain-language summaries of their purpose and potential effects.
- **Promises vs. Actions:** How campaign promises and public statements compare with documented actions.
- **Ethics & Accountability:** Documented ethics history, clearly distinguishing ongoing investigations from confirmed violations.
- **Funder–Legislation Connections:** Compare funders’ public positions on bills with the actions of politicians they support, highlighting shared interests and potential influence without assuming causation.
- **Voter Alignment:** How candidates’ records and stated positions align with users’ policy priorities.

Our goal is to **make political accountability accessible** through clear, source-backed information that helps voters make informed choices.
