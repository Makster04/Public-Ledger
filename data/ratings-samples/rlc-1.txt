2 H.J. Res 35 Support
This joint resolution nullifies an EPA rule from 2024 which imposed a fee/tax on methane and other emmissions from the oil/gas industry. H.J.Res 35 is a good bill to

3
Paul Amendment 1266
to H.R. 1968
Support
The Paul Amendment #1266 to H.R. 1968 would have cut funding to the U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID), eliminating $16 billion in foreign aid as
proposed by DOGE. (Failed Senate 27-73 on 3/14/25)
4 H.R. 1968 Cloture Oppose
The Cloture Motion to H.R. 1968 allowed the Senate to bring this bill to the floor for consideration. H.R. 1968 is the Full-Year Continuing Appropriations and Extensions
Act, which approves $1.6 trillion in spending, funding the federal government from March 15, 2025 through September 30, 2025. This bill continues funding the federal
government at the same levels approved by President Biden during the prior year. With a $38 trillion debt, this bloated spending bill is irresponsible and reckless.
(Passed House 217-213 on 3/11/25, Passed Senate Cloture 62-38 on 3/14/25, Passed Senate 54-46 on 3/14/25, Signed by President)

6
Paul Amendment 1760
to H.Con.Res.14
Support
The Paul Amendment #1760 to H. Con. Res. 14 would have reduced the debt ceiling increase from $4 trillion to $500 billion. With $38 trillion in debt, Congress continues
to kick the can down the road with reckless spending increases. Raising the debt ceiling by $4 trillion only enables Congress to continue this careless pattern. This
amendment would have been a step in the right direction, slowing down the debt increases. (Failed Senate 5-94 on 4/4/25)
7 H.J. Res. 20 Support
This joint resolution nullifies a rule imposed by the Department of Energy in 2024 which imposed onerous requirements on gas-fired water heaters, essentially banning
them. H.J. Res. 20 is a good bill to restore economic freedom by repealing unnecessary and excessive regulations.
(Passed House 221-198 on 2/27/25, Passed Senate 53-44 on 4/10/25, Signed by President)
8 H.J. Res. 87 Support
This joint resolution nullifies an EPA waiver that allowed California to mandate the sale of zero-emmission trucks. In short, H.J. Res. 87 repealed California's mandate
that required car manufacturers to sell zero-emmission trucks. (Passed House 231-191 on 4/30/25, Passed Senate 51-45 on 5/22/25, Signed by President)
9 H.R. 1 Oppose
H.R. 1 appropriates $320 billion in new spending, while increasing the debt ceiling by $5 trillion. It increases the budget deficit by $270 billion. The bill does do some
good things: it extends the 2017 tax cuts, increases certain tax deductions and eliminates taxes on tips and overtime. While we support tax cuts, overrall, this legislation
is fiscally ierresponsible. It significantly increases the size of the federal government while further adding to the $38 trillion national debt.
(Passed House 218-214 on 7/3/25, Passed Senate 51-50 on 7/1/25, Signed by President)
10 H.R. 4 Support
The Rescissions Act of 2025, which rescinds $9.4 billion in federal funding for various agencies, including the Department of State, Corporation for Public Broadcasting,
and the US Agency for International Development. H.R. 4 is a good bill that cuts wasteful, unnecessary spending.
(Passed House 214-212 on 6/12/25, Passed Senate 51-48 on 7/17/25, Signed by President)
11 S. Con. Res. 22 Support
Sen. Rand Paul's "Penny Plan," which balances the budget in five years by enacting a 6% spending cut across the board each year.
(Failed Senate 36-62 on 9/16/25)
12 S. 2882 Oppose
The Continuing Appropriations and Extensions and Other Matters Act. This bill continues funding the federal government for one month, while enacting multiple
spending increases. The bill would have appropriated an additional $491 million for the Corporation for Public Broadasting and an additional $238 million for
Congressional security. With a $38 trillion debt, this bloated spending bill is irresponsible and reckless. (Failed Senate Cloture 47-53 on 9/30/25)
13 H.R. 4016 Cloture Oppose
The Cloture Motion to H.R. 4016 would have allowed the Senate to bring this bill to the floor for consideration. H.R. 4016 is the Department of Defense Appropriations
Act, which appropriates $831.5 billion for the Department of Defense budget for FY2026. This pushes total 2026 military spending above $1 trillion, a 13% increase from
the prior year. While we support a strong military, it is fiscally irresponsible to give this much taxpayer money to a department that has failed its audit eight years in a
row, especially when factoring in the $38 trillion national debt. (Passed House 221-209 on 7/18/25, Senate Cloture Motion Failed 50-44 on 10/16/25)

14 S. J. Res. 88 Support
This joint resolution would terminate the national emergency declared by the President on April 2, 2025 which imposed a 10% tariff on most imports to the United States.
Tariffs are a tax passed down to American consumers, raising prices while violating free market principles. (Passed Senate 51-47 on 10/30/25, Held in House)

3941 to H.R. 5371
Oppose
This motion tabled the Paul Amendment #3941 to H.R. 5371, preventing the amendment from getting a vote. This amendment would have repealed the provision in
H.R. 5371 that bans hemp nationwide. (Motion to Table Passed Senate 76-24 on 11/10/25)
16 H.R. 5371 Cloture Oppose
The Cloture Motion to H.R. 5371 allowed the Senate to bring this bill to the floor for consideration. H.R. 5371 is The Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act,
which funds the federal government from November 12th, 2025 through January 30th, 2026, at the same spending levels approved by President Biden during the prior
year. The bill also includes a full year of funding for the Department of Agriculture, the FDA, the Department of Veterans Affairs, and Congress. The bill also bans hemp
nationwide and allows eight Senators to seek up to $500,000 in damages from the government accessing their phone records. With a $38 trillion debt, this bloated
spending bill is irresponsible and reckless.
(Passed House 222-209 on 11/12/25, Passed Senate Cloture 60-40 on 11/9/25, Passed Senate 60-40 on 11/10/25, Signed by President)
17 H.R. 4405 Support
The Epstein Files Transparency Act, which orders the Department of Justice to publish all the Epstein Files within 30 days of its passage.
(Passed House 427-1 on 11/18/25, Passed Senate 100-0 on 11/18/25, Signed by President)
18 S. 3385 Oppose
The Lower Healthcare Costs Act, which would have extended the Affordable Care Act (Obamacare) tax credits for three years.
(Failed Senate Cloture 51-48 on 12/11/25)
19 S. 1071 Cloture Oppose
The Cloture Motion to S. 1071 allowed the Senate to bring this bill to the floor for consideration. S. 1071 is the final version of the 2025 National Defense Authorization
Act. This 3,086 page bill appropriates $900.6 billion in military spending for FY2026, which is $8 billion more than the President’s budget request. Adding to the $156
billion previously allocated in H.R. 1, this pushes the defense budget over $1 trillion; over $100 billion more than the previous year. The bill allocates an extensive
amount of foreign aid: $1 billion for Taiwan, $2.5 billion for the Philippines, $685 million for Israel, $800 million for Ukraine, and $175 million for Estonia, Latvia, and
Lithuania. While we support a strong military, it is fiscally irresponsible to give this much taxpayer money to a department that has failed its audit eight years in a row,
especially when factoring in the $38 trillion national debt.
(Passed House 312-212 on 12/10/25, Passed Senate Cloture 76-20 on 12/15/25, Passed Senate 77-20 on 12/17/25, Signed by President)
