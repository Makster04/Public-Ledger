1
H.R. 776 Oppose
The Nutria Eradication and Control Reauthorization Act, which reauthorizes through 2030 the Department of Interior to provide financial assistance to states to
eradicate/control nutrial (invasive rodents) and restore marshalnd damaged by nutria. This issue should be left up to the states. With $38 trillion of debt, the federal
government cannot afford this. (Passed House 361-56 on 2/4/25, Held in Senate)
2 H.R. 26 Support
The Protecting American Energy Production Act, which prohibits any U.S. President from declaring a moratorium on hydraulic fracturing (fracking) without authorization
from Congress. The bill also states that fracking should be left up to the states to regulate. H.R. 26 safeguards free enterprise while preventing the executive branch
from side stepping Congress. (Passed House 226-188 on 2/7/25, Held in Senate)
3 H.J. Res 35 Support
This joint resolution nullifies an EPA rule from 2024 which imposed a fee/tax on methane and other emmissions from the oil/gas industry. H.J.Res 35 is a good bill to
repeal an unnecessary tax. ( Passed House 220-206 on 2/26/25, Passed Senate 52-47 on 2/27/25, Signed by President)
4 H.J. Res. 20 Support
This joint resolution nullifies a rule imposed by the Department of Energy in 2024 which imposed onerous requirements on gas-fired water heaters, essentially banning
them. H.J. Res. 20 is a good bill to restore economic freedom by repealing unnecessary and excessive regulations.
(Passed House 221-198 on 2/27/25, Passed Senate 53-44 on 4/10/25, Signed by President)
5 H.R. 1968 Oppose
The Full-Year Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, which approves $1.6 trillion in spending, funding the federal government from March 15, 2025 through
September 30, 2025. This bill continues funding the federal government at the same levels approved by President Biden during the prior year. With a $38 trillion debt,
this bloated spending bill is irresponsible and reckless. (Passed House 217-213 on 3/11/25, Passed Senate 54-46 on 3/14/25, Signed by President)
6 H.R. 1534 Oppose
The IMPACT Act, which requires the Department of Energy to fund a new Research and Development program focused on reducing emmissions from asphalt and
conrete production. With a $38 trillion debt, it is fiscally irresponsible to expand the scope of the Department of Energy.
(Passed House 350-73 on 3/25/25, Held in Senate)
7 H.R. 973 Oppose
The Setting Consumer Standards for Lithium-Ion Batteries Act, which imposes new regulations (consumer standards) on Lithium-Ion Batteries. This bill was drafted with
good intentions: to improve battery safety. However, instead of creating more federal regulations, this issue should be left to the free market.
(Passed House 365-42 on 4/28/25, Held in Senate)
8 H.R. 1402 Oppose
The TICKET Act, which imposes new regulations on event ticket sales. This bill was drafted with good intentions: to offer transparency for ticket buyers. However,
instead of creating more federal regulations, this issue should be left to the free market.
(Passed House 409-15 on 4/29/25, Held in Senate)
9 H.J. Res. 87 Support
This joint resolution nullifies an EPA waiver that allowed California to mandate the sale of zero-emmission trucks. In short, H.J. Res. 87 repealed California's mandate
that required car manufacturers to sell zero-emmission trucks. (Passed House 231-191 on 4/30/25, Passed Senate 51-45 on 5/22/25, Signed by President)
10 H.R. 4 Support
The Rescissions Act of 2025, which rescinds $9.4 billion in federal funding for various agencies, including the Department of State, Corporation for Public Broadcasting,
and the US Agency for International Development. H.R. 4 is a good bill that cuts wasteful, unnecessary spending.
(Passed House 214-212 on 6/12/25, Passed Senate 51-48 on 7/17/25, Signed by President)
11 H.R. 1 Oppose
H.R. 1 appropriates $320 billion in new spending, while increasing the debt ceiling by $5 trillion. It increases the budget deficit by $270 billion. The bill does do some
good things: it extends the 2017 tax cuts, increases certain tax deductions and eliminates taxes on tips and overtime. While we support tax cuts, overrall, this legsilation
is fiscally ierresponsible. It significantly increases the size of the federal government while further adding to the $38 trillion national debt.
(Passed House 218-214 on 7/3/25, Passed Senate 51-50 on 7/1/25, Signed by President)
12 H.R. 4016 Oppose
The Department of Defense Appropriations Act, which appropriates $831.5 billion for the Department of Defense budget for FY2026. This pushes total 2026 military
spending above $1 trillion, a 13% increase from the prior year. While we support a strong military, it is fiscally irresponsible to give this much taxpayer money to a
department that has failed its audit eight years in a row, especially when factoring in the $38 trillion national debt.
(Passed House 221-209 on 7/18/25, Senate Cloture Motion Failed 50-44 on 10/16/25)

15 H.R. 5371 Oppose
The Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, which funds the federal government from November 12th, 2025 through January 30th, 2026, at the same spending
levels approved by President Biden during the prior year. The bill also includes a full year of funding for the Department of Agriculture, the FDA, the Department of
Veterans Affairs, and Congress. The bill also bans hemp nationwide and allows eight Senators to seek up to $500,000 in damages from the government accessing their
phone records. With a $38 trillion debt, this bloated spending bill is irresponsible and reckless.
(Passed House 222-209 on 11/12/25, Passed Senate 60-40 on 11/10/25, Signed by President)
16 H.R. 4405 Support
The Epstein Files Transparency Act, which orders the Department of Justice to publish all the Epstein Files within 30 days of its passage.
(Passed House 427-1 on 11/18/25, Passed Senate 100-0 on 11/18/25, Signed by President)
*Note: In addition to scoring the floor votes, bonus points were awarded to Representatives who signed the disharge petition to bring the bill to the floor.
17 S. 1071 Oppose
The final version of the 2025 National Defense Authorization Act. This 3,086 page bill appropriates $900.6 billion in military spending for FY2026, which is $8 billion
more than the President’s budget request. Adding to the $156 billion previously allocated in H.R. 1, this pushes the defense budget over $1 trillion; over $100 billion
more than the previous year. The bill allocates an extensive amount of foreign aid: $1 billion for Taiwan, $2.5 billion for the Philippines, $685 million for Israel, $800
million for Ukraine, and $175 million for Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania. While we support a strong military, it is fiscally irresponsible to give this much taxpayer money
to a department that has failed its audit eight years in a row, especially when factoring in the $38 trillion national debt.
(Passed House 312-212 on 12/10/25, Passed Senate 77-20 on 12/17/25, Signed by President)
18 H. Con. Res. 64 Support
This concurrent resolution directs the President to remove U.S. Armed Forces from hostilities within or against Venezuela unless a declaration of war or authorization
to use military force has been enacted. (Failed House 211-213 on 12/17/25)
19 H.R. 6703 Support
The Lower Health Care Premiums for All Americans Act, which expands access to association health plans and creates a tax credit for small businesses. This bill
reduces government mandates and increases economic freedom, allowing more affordable options for health insurance; especially for those employed by small
businesses. The Congressional Budget Office estimates that the bill will reduce the deficit by $35.6 million. (Passed House 216-211 on 12/17/25, Held in Senate)
20 H.R. 4776 Support
The SPEED Act, which speeds up the federal permitting process required before building infrastructure, energy, and mining projects. Specifically, the bill curbs some
environmental regulations and shortens the permitting process, allowing these projects to move along faster. (Passed House 221-196 on 12/18/25, Held in Senate)
