AWA enforcement co-sponsor

About:

A indicates co-sponsorship of the Better Collaboration, Accountability, and Regulatory Enforcement (CARE) for Animals Act (S. 1538), which would strengthen collaboration between the U.S. Department of Justice and the U.S. Department of Agriculture to ensure sufficient enforcement of the Animal Welfare Act (AWA) and help deter violations. The AWA sets basic care standards for over a million animals in research, exhibition, commercial breeding and other facilities, yet many licensees are getting away with egregious abuses due to inadequate enforcement of the law. While the DOJ may currently ask federal courts to issue injunctions to prevent continued unlawful mistreatment of animals, the Better CARE for Animals Act would authorize the agency to seek license revocations, civil penalties, and forfeiture to seize and remove suffering animals. It would also codify a memorandum of understanding between the USDA and the DOJ to encourage greater collaboration and ensure the DOJ has access to evidence needed to initiate cases, following related report language in prior appropriations measures. This legislation is endorsed by more than 120 national, state and local animal protection groups; rescues; sanctuaries; and law enforcement organizations, including the National Sheriffs’ Association, Association of Prosecuting Attorneys, and National Animal Care and Control Association.

Sponsors:

Sens. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn.
John Kennedy, R-La.

Leader

About:

A indicates that the member earned extra credit for taking critical action to benefit animals, such as leading as a prime sponsor of pro-animal legislation we endorsed (including items not scored in this chart). Members also earned extra credit for co-signing any of three letters to the Agriculture Appropriations subcommittee for fiscal year 2027 or submitting a corresponding individual request. The first letter sought provisions for enforcement of the AWA, Horse Protection Act, Humane Methods of Slaughter Act and federal animal fighting law, as well as for programs to promote alternatives to animal testing, to provide shelter options for domestic violence survivors with pets through the Protecting Animals With Shelter (PAWS) grant program, and to encourage veterinarians to locate in underserved areas. This letter also included requests to cover companion animal care along with farm animal care in the veterinary incentive program and to prevent the USDA from issuing or renewing AWA licenses for exhibitors that conduct overnight travel with elephants. The second letter urged the subcommittee to reject any language that would block state and local farm animal welfare laws. The third letter asked for a provision to permanently defund USDA horse slaughter inspections and thus prevent horse slaughter plants from ever operating in the U.S. A indicates that the member earned double extra credit for taking two or more of these actions, leading a requested sign-on letter to a federal agency or congressional committee, or leading on a top priority issue counted in this scorecard.

Puppy mills co-sponsor

About:

A indicates co-sponsorship of the Puppy Protection Act (H.R. 2253) to address serious gaps in welfare standards for tens of thousands of breeding dogs and puppies at large-scale breeding operations licensed by the USDA. Although the agency upgraded dog care standards (e.g., requiring hands-on veterinary exams and 24-hour access to clean water) in a May 2020 rulemaking, in many ways the regulations still allow overcrowded, inhumane conditions at puppy mills, which often result in sick puppies being sold to consumers. This bill, which is endorsed by the National Sheriffs’ Association, would codify the 2020 rule’s upgrades and make other overdue reforms, such as prohibiting the use of stacked cages and gridded or wire cage flooring, requiring space for exercise, mandating protection from extreme heat and cold, requiring prompt veterinary care for illness or injury, and requiring that breeders attempt to find humane placement for retired breeding dogs rather than destroying them.

Sponsors:

Reps. Brian Fitzpatrick, R-Pa.
Jim McGovern, D-Mass.
Guy Reschenthaler, R-Pa.
Jimmy Panetta, D-Calif.

Horse slaughter co-sponsor

About:

A indicates co-sponsorship of the Save America’s Forgotten Equines (SAFE) Act (H.R. 1661) to prohibit the reopening of horse slaughter plants in the U.S. and the export of American horses to slaughter for human consumption. Horse slaughter is inherently cruel due to the unique biology of horses, and more than 80% of Americans oppose it. Horses destined for slaughter are shipped long distances without food, water or rest in crowded trucks and are often seriously injured in transit. At the slaughter plant, they often suffer repeated blows to the head during attempts to render them unconscious and are sometimes still alive when dismembered. This predatory industry doesn’t “euthanize” old, sick horses; young and healthy horses are purchased, often by buyers misrepresenting their intentions, and killed for the overseas horsemeat market. American horses aren’t raised for food and are routinely given hundreds of drugs over their lifetimes that can be toxic to humans if ingested.

Sponsors:

Reps. Vern Buchanan, R-Fla.
Jan Schakowsky, D-Ill.

Farm Bill vote

About:

A indicates a vote against passage of the House Farm Bill (H.R. 7567). While representatives had many reasons for voting as they did on this large package, we urged a no vote because the package contained an egregious provision, Section 12006, to nullify state and local farm animal welfare laws in California and other states. This provision, which mirrors the controversial Save Our Bacon Act (H.R. 4673), aims to wipe out laws banning the sale of products from animals kept in such extreme confinement that they’re unable to turn around and move freely. It would also jeopardize hundreds of other state laws relating to disease control, toxic materials, quarantine rules, food labeling and more. A diverse coalition of over 7,200 organizations and individuals have publicly opposed these attacks on state and local agricultural laws, including more than 5,300 farms and 1,200 veterinary professionals.

Sponsors:

Rep. Glenn Thompson, R-Pa.

USDA/FDA animal welfare letter

About:

A indicates that a member was one of the 194 representatives who co-signed a group letter or submitted a corresponding individual request in March 2026 to the appropriations subcommittee that funds the USDA and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration. These letters sought provisions in the fiscal year 2027 funding bill for enforcement of the AWA, Horse Protection Act, Humane Methods of Slaughter Act and federal animal fighting law, as well as for programs to promote alternatives to animal testing, to provide shelter for domestic violence survivors with pets through the Protecting Animals With Shelter (PAWS) grant program, and to encourage veterinarians to locate in underserved areas. These letters included requests to cover companion animal care along with farm animal care in the veterinary incentive program and to prevent the USDA from issuing or renewing AWA licenses for exhibitors that conduct overnight travel with elephants. The letters also requested that no provisions be included attacking state and local farm animal welfare laws.

Sponsors:

Reps. Chris Smith, R-N.J.
Mike Quigley, D-Ill.

Horse slaughter defund letter

About:

A indicates that a member was one of the 154 representatives who co-signed a group letter (or submitted a corresponding individual request) to the appropriations subcommittee that funds the USDA and FDA. The letter requested a provision in the FY 2027 funding bill to permanently prohibit funds being provided for the USDA to inspect horse slaughter plants, thus preventing such plants from ever operating in the U.S.
