# Site updates

Each dated line below becomes a "Site update" card on the home page and the Latest updates page, its bold title as the headline (`scripts/build-news.mjs`, through `public/news.json`, rebuilt daily while `npm start` runs). Write the title as a headline. A maintenance-only line ends with `<!-- not news -->` and is left out. Newest first, under a heading for its month (`## November 2026`).

## November 2026

- **Nov. 26: Super PAC cards for the five lobbies without super PACs of their own.** Big Pharma, Health Insurers + PBMs, Big Oil, Big Defense and Wall Street run no super PACs, so their cards on the Super PACs page now follow their money into the super PACs their companies, PACs and trade groups fund: how much they gave, their share of what those super PACs spent for and against candidates, and how many of the candidates backed by super PACs they gave $100,000 or more won. In 2026, Big Oil's companies gave $53 million, mostly to Senate Leadership Fund, Congressional Leadership Fund and MAGA Inc. Gifts from people who work for those companies are shown beside, not counted, and a share from fewer than 5 candidates isn't shown.
- **Nov. 26: How the trackers' super PACs did in the midterms.** The Super PACs page opens with a card for each of the eight trackers: what its own super PACs spent supporting and opposing House and Senate candidates in the 2026 or 2022 midterms, with bars of how much went to candidates who won and who lost, and the share of the candidates they backed who won, with an arrow against the midterm before. In 2026, Crypto's super PACs backed 97 candidates and 80% won, Big Tech + AI's backed 62 and 79% won, and the Pro-Israel Lobby's United Democracy Project backed 7 and 3 won (43%, down from 78% in 2022). Open, at the bottom of each card, lists its super PACs. Five trackers have no super PAC of their own, and their cards say so.
- **Nov. 26: Super PAC spending is counted once per filing.** Reading the FEC's spending file, a report that an amendment replaces is now left out whole, and separate reports that number their lines the same way are all counted. Before, Congressional Leadership Fund's 2022 spending read as a single line, and Fellowship PAC's 2026 total read $6.5 million instead of $4.8 million, which changes its numbers under Spent to help them win.
- **Nov. 26: A new Big Money Independence bonus: No lobby money.** Taking no corporate PAC money still earns +5, but it only looks at PACs the FEC lists as corporate, so a campaign could earn it while taking hundreds of thousands from a lobby through other PACs and gifts routed through them. A second +5 now goes to campaigns that took nothing this cycle from any of the eight tracked lobbies, the same eight lines as the box beside the score, which is renamed "Tracking Lobby Money" (it was "Tracking Major Super PACs"). 16 people earn it, and 509 took from at least one lobby. The +1 for pledging to take no corporate PAC money moved from Bonuses to Pledges and bills, worth the same, so nobody loses points.
- **Nov. 26: State legislature records can count as stances.** `npm run state:export` copies the state bills the refresh collected (Open States and LegiScan) for the 28 members-elect who served in a state legislature, and others who did, into `data/state-records/`; `npm run state:acts` turns the bills tagged to a question into stances on it, counted like a member of Congress's bills and votes. Nothing counts until the bills are tagged and approved. <!-- not news -->
