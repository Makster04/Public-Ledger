# How Public Ledger connects dark money to legislation

What dark money is, and what the site can and can't show about it: see [.03_DarkMoney-Politicians.md](.03_DarkMoney-Politicians.md).

## Where it lives: the Legislation tab

A card for each of the 49 bills the site follows, and a page for each bill (`public/legislation-page.js`).

## The chain the site shows, one public record per link

1. **A group's side on a bill:** its own statements, key-vote letters and scorecards, each linked.
2. **Its money:** what it spent to help or against candidates, the gifts it forwarded, and its PACs' gifts in the races tracked here (FEC). This is the group's total across those races, not money spent on that bill.
3. **How open its money is:** a "lists few donors" flag when an outside spender behind it files with the FEC as a group, not a PAC (an FEC ID starting with C9), so it reports only gifts given for its ads.
4. **The vote:** each roll call with each party's split, and the lawmakers on each side.
5. **Whether the lawmakers it helped voted its way:** each lawmaker's newest Yea or Nay on the bill. A group for the bill wants Yea and one against it wants Nay. Motions to table, recommit, postpone or reconsider are skipped, because Yea there can mean the opposite, unless the group's side came from that very roll call.

**Also on each bill:** where it stands, with sources; its People First score (half from a nonpartisan study of who gains, half from which worker and business groups took sides); its sponsors and cosponsors on this site; lobbying counts from LDA.gov; each group's nonprofit tax return (its political spending, the political groups it paid and the grants it got from other nonprofits followed here); and the lobbying coalitions among the reports' clients, with the member organizations each lists on its registration (each pays more than $5,000 a quarter toward the lobbying).

## Example

America's Credit Unions, which files with the FEC as a group, spent $754K to help Susan Collins. It publicly backed the GENIUS Act, and Collins voted Yea on June 17, 2025. So did 96.2% of Senate Republicans, so the vote says more about her party than about the money.

## On profiles

People First's Corporation Compromised applies the same idea to each politician: how much more often they vote with their business donors than the rest of their party does, and how often they took the profit side on the scored bills those donors lobbied on (LDA.gov reports, including coalitions the donors belong to; `npm run lobbying:bills`). Each profile also shows lobbyists' gifts that name them as the one honored (LDA.gov LD-203), with lobbyists added up and lobbying organizations named.

## What it can't show

That money changed a vote, or who funded a group that hides its donors.
